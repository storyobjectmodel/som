## Core (8)

| Term | Status | Definition | See also / Issues |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Asset** | CONFIRMED | A discrete piece of media or editorial work classified by editorial position: primary, secondary, or tertiary. These are editorial classifications, NOT computational. Assets exist as completed objects whether or not any telling is produced. Classification is load-bearing for compliance: (asset category × outlet/path) → skills. Assets are themselves endpoints — they exist independently, can be killed, can sit unused, can be reused later. — TERM CONFLICT on scope: On Friday (hackathon), 'asset' meant a raw upstream component. By 17 May, the term expanded to cover ALL classified objects (raw and editorial). Schema authors flagged the scope shift on 17 May and resolved with a three-stage sequence: assets, packages, tellings. Confirmed that assets have always been broad and portable. A transcode of a primary clip is still primary — no editorial work has been done. A correspondent's package is secondary regardless of file lineage. Without the classification in the schema, the skills firing rule has nothing to anchor to. | Content, Telling, Primary Source, Secondary Source, Tertiary Source, Skills Firing Rule, Asset-to-Telling Transition · SOM-004, SOM-005 |
| **Asset-to-Telling Transition** | PROPOSED | The cross-point where a completed asset meets an outlet — the telling is created at runtime by e.g. the publishing system. This is the compliance firing event: (asset category × outlet/path) → skills. — Before this cross-point, the editorial work exists as a completed asset. After it, a telling exists carrying the asset + outlet relationship. | Asset, Telling, Skills Firing Rule, Destination · SOM-055 |
| **Story** | OPEN | The happening — the underlying reportable event or situation. Primary sources are evidence OF the happening, not the happening itself. | **Story Object** | OPEN | The full persistent entity representing a story across its entire lifecycle. Janet's Question #1: does SOM own the Story Object or just Story Context at a point in time? | Story, Story Context · — |
